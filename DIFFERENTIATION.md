# How Tollwarden differs from agent trust scoring and proof-of-execution services

Existing "agent trust" services answer the question *"is this counterparty generally trustworthy?"* — they score identities using historical behavior, stake, attestations, or verified execution traces, and proof-of-execution systems answer *"did the service actually do the work I paid for?"* after the fact. Tollwarden answers a different question at a different moment: *"is this specific payment, right now, safe to transmit?"* It is a transaction-level firewall, not an identity-level score — it inspects the payment payload itself (does the metadata leak a seed phrase or API key?), the payment's provenance (did the decision to pay originate from a prompt-injected tool result rather than the agent's own plan?), its mechanics (is this nonce being replayed? is the amount 10× the quoted price?), and its destination's structure (homoglyph domain, IP literal, credential demand) — all *before* settlement, when the money can still be kept. A counterparty with a flawless reputation score can still be paid via a replayed authorization, at an inflated price, with a customer's PII in the description, on the instruction of a poisoned webpage; conversely, a brand-new counterparty with no reputation at all can be a perfectly safe recipient of a clean payment. Reputation in Tollwarden exists only as one signal among many — a shared post-hoc incident registry, not a score to optimize — and the whole service is deliberately non-custodial and advisory, wrapping the wallet/facilitator stack the agent already uses rather than replacing any part of it.

Three additions (2026-07-16) sharpen the separation. First, the **verdict-to-enforcement chain**: every verdict is Ed25519-signed and bound to `sha256(network|pay_to|asset|amount|nonce)`, so a wallet can *physically refuse* to sign any authorization lacking a fresh allow-verdict for exactly that payment — scores advise, Tollwarden verdicts can be enforced. Second, **human-in-the-loop step-up approvals** complete that chain: a flag pauses for a human, whose one click mints a short-lived override carrying a distinct `override:allow` tag in the signed message itself — auditable, payment-bound, and accepted by the enforcement kit only by explicit opt-in. Third, the **delivery-outcome ledger** takes the useful half of proof-of-execution at a fraction of its cost: because x402 delivery is synchronous, Tollwarden's payment-path wrappers mechanically observe whether the paid response actually contained the goods and record it — bound to a scan Tollwarden performed, so delivery history cannot be Sybil-farmed without making real payments. That is not a cryptographic proof the work was *correct* (proof-of-execution's territory); it is measured evidence the seller *ships*, captured automatically, feeding a flag-only check on every future payment to that seller. Identity scores tell you who has behaved; proofs tell you what was done; Tollwarden decides, enforces, escalates, and then verifies the one thing agents actually buy: delivery.

Three more moves (2026-07-21) extend the chain at both ends. At the wallet end, **local policy**: the enforcement kit now takes a hard recipient allowlist and per-payment/cumulative spend caps, checked against the EIP-712 typed data at signature time with no server involved and independent of any verdict — so even if every advisory layer above it is confused or compromised, the wallet can only move bounded amounts to known parties (a human-approved override can admit one commitment-bound payment past the allowlist, never a recipient, and never past the caps). At the evidence end, the delivery ledger now publishes its own **denominator**: reputation lookups report `scans_seen` and `report_coverage` next to the outcome counts, so a selectively reported track record reads as low coverage instead of passing as a complete record — an honesty property most scoring systems cannot state about their own inputs, because they don't control when the inputs were committed. And the identity leg went on-chain: Tollwarden is a registered **ERC-8004 agent** (agentId 59586 on Base, registration file cross-linked with the on-chain tokenURI), positioning the verdict layer inside the emerging agent-trust standard — with on-chain validation attestations as the planned next step once that portion of the spec stabilizes.
