# How PaySafe differs from agent trust scoring and proof-of-execution services

Existing "agent trust" services answer the question *"is this counterparty generally trustworthy?"* — they score identities using historical behavior, stake, attestations, or verified execution traces, and proof-of-execution systems answer *"did the service actually do the work I paid for?"* after the fact. PaySafe answers a different question at a different moment: *"is this specific payment, right now, safe to transmit?"* It is a transaction-level firewall, not an identity-level score — it inspects the payment payload itself (does the metadata leak a seed phrase or API key?), the payment's provenance (did the decision to pay originate from a prompt-injected tool result rather than the agent's own plan?), its mechanics (is this nonce being replayed? is the amount 10× the quoted price?), and its destination's structure (homoglyph domain, IP literal, credential demand) — all *before* settlement, when the money can still be kept. A counterparty with a flawless reputation score can still be paid via a replayed authorization, at an inflated price, with a customer's PII in the description, on the instruction of a poisoned webpage; conversely, a brand-new counterparty with no reputation at all can be a perfectly safe recipient of a clean payment. Reputation in PaySafe exists only as one signal among many — a shared post-hoc incident registry, not a score to optimize — and the whole service is deliberately non-custodial and advisory, wrapping the wallet/facilitator stack the agent already uses rather than replacing any part of it.
